Looking back at my bash history and `agy` (Antigravity CLI) sessions, my Arch Linux setup on this **ThinkPad X13 Gen 1** wasn't a clean weekend install—it was a chain of tiny rabbit holes that slowly turned a thin business laptop into my favorite daily driver.

## Act I: From Bare TTY to *illogical-impulse*

It started in a minimal terminal: installing `mesa`, `vulkan-intel`, and `intel-media-driver` for the Comet Lake iGPU, wiring up `pipewire` and `wireplumber`, and compiling `yay-bin` from the AUR.

At first I launched raw `hyprland` (even got the autogenerated `hyprland.lua` config), then realized I wanted proper systemd session management (`uwsm start hyprland` + `sddm`) and a desktop shell that didn't look barren. That's when I cloned `end-4/dots-hyprland` (*illogical-impulse*).

Almost immediately I started trimming it to fit my workflow—dropping `kitty`, `dolphin`, and extra KDE integration packages (`illogical-impulse-kde`) so I could live inside **`ghostty`** and **`yazi`**.

## Act II: Hardware Quirks & The Brightness Keybind Battle

Every Arch install has its humbling moments. Mine were wireless drivers and Lua syntax:

* **Wireless & Bluetooth:** After poking through `iwlwifi`, `rfkill`, and manual `wpa_supplicant` + `systemd-networkd` experiments, I settled cleanly on `NetworkManager`. When Bluetooth peripherals refused to cooperate, `sudo modprobe uhid` finally brought HID devices to life.
* **The `SUPER + ALT + Up/Down` Saga:** I wanted keyboard-centered brightness shortcuts without stretching to the function row. First I appended classic `.conf` `bindel` lines to `hyprland.conf`—nothing. Then I hit permissions friction (`chown`), discovered *illogical-impulse* uses **Lua** (`keybinds.lua`) on Hyprland `0.57.2`, and ran straight into `hyprctl configerrors` because `"SUPER ALT, Up"` needed `+`-separated keys:

```lua
hl.bind("SUPER + ALT + Up", hl.dsp.exec_cmd(qsIpcCall .. " brightness increment || brightnessctl s 5%+"), { flags = "el" })
hl.bind("SUPER + ALT + Down", hl.dsp.exec_cmd(qsIpcCall .. " brightness decrement || brightnessctl s 5%-"), { flags = "el" })
```

* **Stopping Mid-Work Suspends:** My laptop kept sleeping during long tasks, so I edited `hypridle.conf` to push lock and screen-off out to 20 minutes and removed the 15-minute auto-suspend completely.

Along the way, poking through `keybinds.lua` also introduced me to built-ins I now use constantly: `SUPER + Slash` (cheatsheet), `SUPER + SHIFT + S/X/C/R` (snip, OCR, color picker, region recording), and `SUPER + V` (clipboard history).

## Act III: Asking `agy` What This Machine Can Really Do

Once the desktop felt like home, `agy` became my sounding board for pushing the ThinkPad further:

1. **Future Upgrades:** We mapped out a ThinkPad X13 Gen 1 wishlist—a single-sided `M.2 2280` NVMe upgrade, a `2242` SSD in the WWAN slot, a 400-nit low-power 1080p panel, `PTM7950` thermal pad, and replacing my 799-cycle `L19M3P72` battery (down to ~75% health).
2. **Real-World Audit:** Even with a 4-core/8-thread `i5-10310U` and `15 GiB` RAM, idle usage sits around `~3.3 GiB`, leaving plenty of room for Docker, Python/Node/C/Rust toolchains, and cloud coding agents (`agy`, `claude`, `orca`).
3. **Content Creation Surprise:** Checking my media stack revealed `xdg-desktop-portal-hyprland`, `wf-recorder`, and Intel Quick Sync (`h264_vaapi` via `intel-media-driver`) were already ready—making 1080p light editing and 1080p30/720p60 OBS coding streams totally viable as long as I stay on AC power and skip software `x264`.

## Epilogue: Turning Notes into This Blog
